Nursia · Marketing & Growth · Sprint PRD

We cannot grow what we cannot measure.

Three systems are watching and none of them sees a whole customer. GA4 and the Meta Pixel run on nursia.io; PostHog runs on app.nursia.io; the redirect between them discards the query string, so no identifier survives the hop. The event every campaign is supposed to bid toward, CompleteRegistration, exists in neither. Until that is fixed, every budget decision is a guess wearing a number. This PRD specifies the estate area by area, sets numeric gates for done, names the technical dependencies, and adds the performance budget those pages have to stay inside while it happens.

Findings

What the audit turned up

Every claim below was verified against the repository and against live PostHog data in project 595036 on 11 September 2026. Nothing here is hypothetical.

Finding 01 · Security · Fix today

Live auth tokens are being stored in analytics.

Supabase returns its session in the URL fragment after OAuth and magic-link signin. The PostHog SDK on app.nursia.io captures $current_url verbatim, fragment included. In the last 30 days: 49 stored events contain an access_token and a refresh_token, and 42 contain a Google provider_token, across 24 people.

A refresh token is a durable credential. Anyone with read access to the PostHog project — staff, contractor, or anyone who obtains a project API key — can lift a session and act as that user, and the Google provider token reaches beyond Nursia entirely. Account emails are captured alongside them in the same strings.

This is not an attribution problem and it does not wait for the sprint. Strip the fragment in the SDK's sanitize_properties (or before_send) hook on app.nursia.io, then purge the affected events and rotate anything exposed. Owned by the app team; raised here because this audit is what surfaced it.

Finding 02 · Attribution · Ship first

The redirect throws away the query string.

src/app/(funnel)/signup/page.tsx is four lines: redirect("https://app.nursia.io"). Next issues a 307 to exactly that URL, and nothing on the incoming URL survives. CtaLink collects five UTMs plus gclid and fbclid on the landing page; all of it dies one hop later.

ad click · gclid + fbclid→ /lp/meta · CtaLink collects→ /signup?utm_source=…&fbclid=…→ 307 · params dropped→ app.nursia.io · anonymous

Google Ads sees no gclid at registration. Meta never sets _fbc on the app domain. GA4 counts the app as a separate session referred by your own marketing site — which is also why organic and paid currently look closer than they are.

Finding 03 · The target event · Ship first

CompleteRegistration does not exist in any system.

You have named it the optimisation target. Today it fires nowhere.

On nursia.io, signedUp() in src/lib/analytics.ts fires the GA4 sign_up, the Google Ads primary conversion and Meta's CompleteRegistration — and its only call site is SignupForm, which lives behind the /signup redirect stub and never mounts.

On app.nursia.io, where registration actually happens, PostHog's event list contains login_viewed, login_completed, onboarding_completed and qualify_lead — and no registration event at all. Signups are demonstrably occurring: the captured callback URLs carry type=signup. They are simply not recorded as anything.

Finding 04 · Split brain

Each tool sees exactly half the journey.

PostHog's only $host value is app.nursia.io — it has never seen the marketing site. GA4, the Meta Pixel and Clarity are installed only in this repo's root layout, so they have never seen the product.

The consequence is that no single system can express the funnel this sprint exists to measure. That is also the opportunity: PostHog is already live on the side where activation and high intent happen, with five days of real data. Adding posthog-js to nursia.io is the cheapest route to one complete funnel — cheaper than teaching GA4 to cross the boundary.

Finding 05 · Scope change · Decide deliberately

Moving questions into the app inverts the funnel.

Questions are being withdrawn from nursia.io. The measurement loss is small — FREE_PREVIEW = 2 capped the public preview at two, so the activation threshold of five was never reachable there, and GA4 was recording a teaser rather than practice. Losing it costs little and makes PostHog the single source, which the rest of this document already assumes.

The consequence that matters is order. If questions exist only in the app, they sit behind registration:

today · pageview→question→registration→activated
after · pageview→registration→question→activated

That deletes the only pre-registration engagement signal there is. The segment it measured — tried it, did not sign up — is exactly the one that separates "the offer is wrong" from "the form is broken", and it is the denominator half of two outcome rows below.

Three knock-on effects. Meta loses its mid-funnel signal: the plan sent question_answered as ViewContent because CompleteRegistration alone is too sparse for delivery to learn from early, and app-only questions leave nothing between PageView and registration. The Ads secondary conversion dies: NEXT_PUBLIC_ADS_QUESTION_LABEL fires inside questionAnswered, so that action joins the primary at zero. And the landing pages lose their demo: QuestionSet renders on twelve page types including all six LPs, where "pick an answer — the rationale opens underneath" is the offer. src/lib/content.ts states the premise plainly: the SEO pages are the product, and every public page ships real questions in static HTML.

None of that argues the decision is wrong — maintaining two question implementations has a real cost. It argues the decision is a landing-page and SEO change as much as a measurement one, and should be priced as such.

Finding 06 · Performance

The app entry page is in Google's “poor” band for LCP.

PostHog's $web_vitals stream gives a real baseline rather than a lab estimate. app.nursia.io/ has a p75 LCP of 4,461 ms against a 2,500 ms “good” threshold, and a p75 CLS of 0.163 against 0.1. Every paid click lands on a page one hop away from that.

Instrumentation adds scripts, and scripts cost LCP. So the performance budget is written into this PRD as a gate rather than a footnote: no ticket ships if it moves p75 LCP the wrong way.

Two decisions, and only one of them is open

Reporting is settled: the dashboards are built in PostHog. That is a business requirement, not a technical preference — it is where the funnel, the activation rates and the web-vitals gate are specified, and where the team will actually look on a Monday.

Collection is engineering's call, and GA4 is an acceptable answer. If the team can genuinely maintain cross-domain unified analytics in GA4 — the linker, the domain list, the session stitching, and the discipline to keep them working — then take that route. PostHog then serves as the compilation layer: the place every event is assembled into one person and one funnel, and the place the dashboards are built.

One implication is worth stating plainly rather than discovering in week three: a dashboard can only report on data the system holds. So PostHog has to receive the funnel events whichever route is chosen. The open question is therefore narrower than it first looks — it is about which system resolves identity across the domain boundary, not about which system the events land in. If GA4 wins the identity question, PostHog still needs the same events alongside it.

How the budget survives four analytics scripts

GA4, the Meta Pixel, Clarity and now PostHog all want to load on the marketing site. Every one of them is afterInteractive or later, never beforeInteractive — the Clarity tag in the root layout is currently beforeInteractive and should move, which is its own ticket. PostHog loads through a same-origin reverse proxy, which also gets it past the blockers that would otherwise bias paid traffic against organic. The LP hero stays server-rendered text and an interactive question, with no video and no client-side image work above the fold.

Decision brief · Area I

Google Tag Manager: engineering's call

Raised as a way to protect Core Web Vitals. That specific reasoning does not hold — a container makes load performance worse, not better — but GTM has real benefits that have nothing to do with speed, and there are ways to load it that blunt most of the cost. This section lays out the trade and the options. The final decision sits with engineering.

First, the thing that is not true

A container does not replace tags, it loads them. The browser fetches gtm.js (tens of kilobytes before a single tag), parses the container, evaluates triggers, and only then injects gtag.js, fbevents.js and the rest. Those tags still execute on the main thread, just later and behind an extra round trip. Adopting GTM to improve Core Web Vitals means adding a script in front of every script already loading.

The real case for it, which is a good one

Autonomy. Marketing adds or changes a pixel without an engineer, a pull request and a deploy. Consent logic, campaign-specific tags and short-lived measurement pixels stop consuming engineering time. If the tag list is about to grow — new ad platforms, affiliate pixels, a consent management platform — that benefit compounds, and it is worth paying some performance for.

The risk to weigh, and it is not speed

A container concentrates failure onto the traffic you are measuring.

googletagmanager.com is among the most heavily blocked domains on the web. Today a blocked GA4 request does not take down the Meta Pixel, and neither touches PostHog — failures are independent and partial. Behind one container, one blocked request removes every tag at once.

That failure is not random: it concentrates in privacy-conscious, mobile and paid traffic, which is exactly the segment DOD-1 and DOD-3 count. This is the item to weigh hardest, and unlike the speed cost it is largely fixable — see the first-party option below.

For engineering to explore

Ways to load GTM without paying full price

If the autonomy case wins, these are the approaches worth prototyping. They are not mutually exclusive; the strongest setup combines the first two.

Server-side GTM

A container running on infrastructure you own. The browser makes one first-party request; tags fire server-side and never ship JavaScript to the page. This is the only option that genuinely removes main-thread work rather than rescheduling it, and it solves the blocking problem at the same time.

Best outcome · highest setup cost · needs a hosting decision

First-party container serving

Serve gtm.js from your own domain through a Next.js rewrite — the same technique NUR-05 uses for PostHog. Does nothing for main-thread cost, but removes the correlated-blocking risk above, which is the more serious of the two problems.

Cheap · fixes blocking, not speed · do this either way

Deferred container load

Load the container after LCP has settled — on requestIdleCallback, first interaction, or a short timer — via next/script's lazyOnload. Keeps the container off the critical path entirely. The cost is real: events before the load are missed unless queued, and bounced sessions may never fire at all.

Easy · measurable speed win · costs early events

Web worker (Partytown)

Moves third-party scripts off the main thread into a worker. Next.js exposes this as an experimental worker script strategy. Attractive on paper and fragile in practice: tags that touch the DOM or cookies frequently break, and debugging is unpleasant. Prototype before committing to it.

Experimental · verify every tag still fires

A deliberately small container

Most GTM performance complaints are really container-bloat complaints. No custom HTML tags, no unused triggers, no vendor templates kept "just in case", and a periodic prune. A lean container costs a fraction of a typical one.

Free · requires ongoing discipline

Container in front of everything

Migrating GA4, Ads, Clarity, the Meta Pixel and PostHog behind one client-side container. This is the configuration that is both slowest and most fragile, and it defeats NUR-05's same-origin proxy. If GTM is adopted, keep PostHog outside it.

Not recommended in any scenario

Recommendation, for engineering to accept or overrule

Not this sprint. With four stable tags and engineers already in the loop, the autonomy benefit is not yet being paid for, and the sprint has a hard LCP gate running through it. Revisit when the tag list approaches ten, or when non-engineers genuinely need to ship pixels — and when you do, evaluate server-side GTM with first-party serving rather than a plain client container, since that combination is the one that avoids both problems above.

Whatever is decided, two things hold: src/app/layout.tsx:138 loads Clarity beforeInteractive, ahead of hydration, and fixing that (NUR-21) is worth more than any tag-management change; and PostHog stays on its own same-origin path regardless, because DOD-3 depends on it.

And separately: tags are not why the app entry page is slow

A p75 LCP of 4,461 ms is not recoverable by deferring analytics. Scripts loaded afterInteractive compete for bandwidth but do not block the largest contentful paint; removing every tag on that page would not bring it under 2,500 ms. The cause is the hero render, an unoptimised image, or server response time. That is C-12, and treating it as a tagging problem would spend the sprint in the wrong place.

P0 product requirements

Three things that are requirements, not options

These are not engineering trade-offs to be weighed. They are the conditions under which the rest of the document can be true.

P0 · Requirement 01

Login lives on nursia.io, not app.nursia.io.

Today src/app/(funnel)/login/page.tsx is a redirect stub to the app. That puts the single most identity-rich moment in the journey on the far side of the boundary that destroys identity.

Bringing login back to nursia.io is worth more to measurement than any other single change in this document. It is the point at which an anonymous browser becomes a known person, and it is the natural place to call identify — on the domain that still holds the first-touch attribution, the _fbc cookie and the click ids. Do it there and the whole cross-domain identity problem shrinks to a handoff of an already-identified user, which is a far easier thing to get right than stitching two anonymous halves together afterwards.

It also fixes Area D on its own: login_viewed at 101 against login_completed at 1 is currently unexplainable because the completion happens somewhere nothing is watching.

P0 · Requirement 02

Session recording on the marketing site.

PostHog session replay is already enabled on the product app. It is not on nursia.io, which is where every paid click lands and where the drop-off this sprint is trying to explain actually happens.

This is the difference between a number and a reason. The funnel will tell you that Meta traffic drops at the gate; a replay tells you whether they scrolled past it, tapped it and bounced, or never saw it. Clarity covers some of this today, but it cannot be filtered by channel or joined to a person, which makes it useless for exactly the question being asked. Enable it with NUR-05, sampled rather than at 100%, and with the same fragment sanitiser NUR-00 installs.

P0 · Requirement 03

The token leak is fixed before anything else ships.

Restated here because it is a requirement and not a ticket to be sequenced. 49 stored events hold a live access_token and refresh_token; 42 hold a Google provider_token. Sanitise, purge, rotate. Nothing else in this document should ship first, and no data export or warehouse connection should run until it is done — an export would replicate a credential leak into a second system with its own access model.

What is not an engineering call

Three things are settled and should not be reopened at ticket level. Dashboards are built in PostHog. The Google Ads conversions stay on gtag — Smart Bidding reads them and nothing else does. And the six gates in the next section are the definition of done; if one is judged unreachable, that is a conversation about the gate, not a ticket that quietly ships without meeting it.

Definition of done

Six numeric gates. All six, or the sprint is not done.

Each gate is measurable the day it is claimed, from a named surface. “It looks right in DebugView” is not one of them.

DOD-1 · ATTRIBUTION ≥ 75%

Share of registration_completed events carrying a resolvable channel — a UTM pair, a gclid, or an fbclid. Not Direct, not Unassigned.

Now: 0% — the event does not fire
DOD-2 · SIGNAL QUALITY ≥ 7.5

Meta Event Match Quality on CompleteRegistration, read in Events Manager. Driven by hashed email, phone, _fbc, _fbp, IP and user agent on the server event.

Now: n/a — no server events
DOD-3 · COVERAGE ≥ 75%

Meta-recorded CompleteRegistration as a share of PostHog registration_completed over the same window. PostHog is the denominator and the source of truth.

Now: undefined — both sides missing
DOD-4 · DEDUPLICATION ± 2%

Pixel and CAPI must collapse to one conversion. Meta's total may not exceed PostHog's by more than 2%, which is the test that catches a broken event_id.

Now: n/a
DOD-5 · PERFORMANCE ≤ +100 ms

Change in p75 LCP on any instrumented surface, measured on real field data seven days after release. A regression past this reverts the ticket.

Baseline: 4461 ms app entry
DOD-6 · HYGIENE 0

Stored events containing access_token, refresh_token or provider_token. Verified by the same query that found them.

Now: 49 / 49 / 42

Why 75% and not 95%

Ad blockers, ITP, and people who never consent all remove conversions that no amount of engineering recovers. A realistic ceiling for browser-plus-server capture on consumer traffic is the high eighties; 75% is the line below which the channel comparison stops being trustworthy, because the missing quarter is not missing at random — it skews toward paid, privacy-conscious and mobile traffic. Treat 75% as the floor for believing the dashboard, not as the target to stop at.

Person properties, not event properties

Set the first-touch channel as a person property (initial_utm_source, initial_utm_medium, initial_fbclid) on first sight, and never overwrite it. PostHog's person-on-events mode stamps the value as it was at ingestion, so a channel written once at first touch stays readable on every later event — including an activation that happens a week and three sessions after the click that paid for it. Without this, breaking a funnel by channel silently attributes activation to whatever the last session's source happened to be.

Reference implementation

The redirect stub, fixed

Four files, one shape. It keeps its job and stops discarding the reason people arrived.

// src/app/(funnel)/signup/page.tsx
import { redirect } from "next/navigation";

const APP = "https://app.nursia.io";

/* Everything the ad platforms and our own funnel need on the far
   side. A bare redirect() drops all of it, which is how a campaign
   ends up bidding toward a conversion it cannot attribute. */
const CARRY = [
  "utm_source", "utm_medium", "utm_campaign", "utm_content", "utm_term",
  "gclid", "fbclid", "src", "guide", "resource", "experiment", "variant",
] as const;

export default async function SignupPage({
  searchParams,
}: {
  searchParams: Promise<Record<string, string | string[] | undefined>>;
}) {
  const from = await searchParams;
  const out = new URLSearchParams();
  for (const key of CARRY) {
    const v = from[key];
    const one = Array.isArray(v) ? v[0] : v;
    if (one) out.set(key, one);
  }
  const qs = out.toString();
  redirect(qs ? `${APP}/signup?${qs}` : `${APP}/signup`);
}

Implementation spec · NUR-05, NUR-06

PostHog on the marketing site

The single change that makes eight of the outcome rows answerable. The product app has been reporting to project 595036 for a while; this side has not, which is why neither half can describe a whole customer. Written out here because the order and the option values both matter, and because two of them are easy to get wrong in ways that cost money rather than throwing an error.

Step 1 — Serve it from our own origin

Two rewrites in next.config.ts, plus skipTrailingSlashRedirect: true. The asset host and the ingestion host are different and must stay so: the SDK bundle and the replay recorder come from us-assets.i.posthog.com, everything else from us.i.posthog.com. Pointing both at one destination silently breaks one of them.

Why the proxy is not optional

Blocking is not evenly distributed, and it follows the comparison.

us.i.posthog.com is on every blocklist worth the name. Paid, mobile and privacy-conscious traffic is blocked at a far higher rate than organic desktop traffic. A dashboard whose entire job is comparing paid against organic therefore cannot use a third-party host: part of the gap it reports would be real and part would be an artefact of who runs a blocker, with no way to separate the two. Same-origin requests are not blocked. This is also what DOD-3's 75% coverage floor depends on.

Step 2 — First touch, written once

Read utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, utm_term, gclid, gbraid, wbraid, fbclid and src from the landing URL, prefix them initial_, and store them with register_once.

PostHog sets its own $initial_* properties and they are not sufficient alone: a reader who arrives from an ad, leaves, and returns a week later through search has a first touch the browser has already forgotten. More to the point, activation happens days and several sessions after the click that paid for it, and person-on-events stamps values as they were at ingestion — so a channel written once at first touch stays readable on the activation event itself.

The mistake to avoid

Use register_once, not setPersonProperties.

setPersonProperties is the obvious-looking call and the wrong one under identified_only: it either does nothing, or it creates the person profile for an anonymous reader that the setting exists to avoid. register_once stores super properties — written only if absent, so "first touch wins" is true at the storage layer rather than something the code has to remember; attached to every later event from that browser; carried through the eventual $identify; and costing no profile in the meantime.

Step 3 — Dual-send the events that already exist

Every analytics call on this site funnels through one track() in src/lib/analytics.ts. Adding a PostHog capture inside it sends all nine existing events — guide_viewed, resource_offered, resource_clicked, resource_unlocked, question_answered and the rest — with no change to any of the nine call sites. That single-funnel design is why this step is one edit rather than a sweep.

Step 4 — Environment and verification

NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_KEY holds the project token for NCLEX-Nursia (595036). It is public by design, exactly like the GA4 and Meta pixel ids already in .env.example — what protects the project is that an ingestion token cannot read data back.

Gate the provider on the key alone, not on NODE_ENV === "production" as the other three tags are. PostHog is now the system of record, and being unable to see events in development is precisely how instrumentation ships broken — which is the failure this document exists to document.

Conversion events · Areas C and H

Purchase, and how it reaches each platform

Registration is the optimisation target. Purchase is the event that says whether the target was worth optimising toward — and it happens on app.nursia.io, several sessions after the click, where no browser tag from this repo will ever run.

PlatformEventTransportKeyed onWhy this transport
PostHog purchase SDK, client person id System of record. Carries value, currency and plan, against a person who already holds the first-touch channel.
Meta Purchase CAPI, server _fbc / _fbp / hashed email The browser is not trustworthy for a payment event and iOS strips most of it. Server-side is the only path that reaches DOD-2.
Google Ads Purchase Offline Conversion Import gclid / gbraid / wbraid The conversion happens where the Ads tag never loads, hours or days after the click. OCI is built for exactly that.
GA4 purchase Measurement Protocol client_id Optional, and only worth it once cross-domain measurement is configured. Otherwise the session is already broken.

Google Ads: an upload, not a container

This does not reopen the GTM decision. That rejection was about a client-side container loading tags in a browser. What purchase needs is the opposite of a browser tag: an upload that never touches one.

Persist gclid at first touch — NUR-01 forwards it, C-02 stores it — then upload the conversion through the Google Ads API with the click id, the conversion action, the timestamp and the value. It is immune to ad blockers, immune to ITP, and works however many days later the purchase lands. Enhanced Conversions for Web is the fallback for purchases where no click id survived, matching on hashed email instead.

One thing the current CtaLink misses: iOS app-campaign traffic arrives carrying gbraid or wbraid rather than gclid. Both have to be carried and stored, or every iOS purchase is unattributable no matter how good the upload is.

One event_id, four destinations

The id generated for the PostHog event is also the Meta event_id and the Ads order_id. It is what stops a purchase counting twice when both the browser and the server report it, and what lets the four systems be reconciled against each other afterwards — which is the whole of DOD-3 and DOD-4.
